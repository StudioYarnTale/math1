Meet the YarnTale Family

Anastasia

Curious, brave, and full of ideas. This is her world — and her adventures begin here.
Anastasia is a bright, curious, and energetic girl — the granddaughter of Vasily Voldemarovich. She is interested in everything: questions, people, ideas, and the world around her. She has a strong character and remarkable determination; when Anastasia wants something, she almost always finds a way to achieve it.
She is not afraid to be active, to ask questions, or to try new things. At times her emotions overflow, and she has not yet learned when it is time to stop or pause. Yet this very emotional openness is her strength — Anastasia lives fully and sincerely, not halfway.
Her path is to learn how to listen not only to her desire to move forward, but also to recognise the moment when it is time to slow down, reflect, and consciously choose her direction.

Lord Vasily Voldemarovich

Wise and kind. He teaches patience, thought, and courage.
Lord Vasily Voldemarovich is a dignified man in his later years — a retired officer with silver hair, a calm gaze, and the quiet nobility of someone who has lived a long and honourable life. His wife passed away long ago, and his children are grown. He lives alone — self-contained, thoughtful, and at peace with himself. Throughout his career, he served faithfully, often facing difficult and unpopular decisions. Duty always came first. Yet behind his strict bearing lies a gentle heart and a deep love for all living beings.
After retirement, he chose a different life — one in harmony with nature. He purchased an abandoned estate and, together with Uncle Alex and Aunty Paulina, restored it piece by piece. The house was rebuilt, the farm repaired, the overgrown yard cleared. In spring, the estate came alive again: crowing, quacking, and grunting filled the air. Piglets wagged their tails at the sight of him, and Arthur the rooster saluted proudly with a triumphant “Cock-a-doodle-doo!”
Vasily Voldemarovich treats all living creatures with reverence. He steps carefully around even the smallest insect. He can walk for hours through fields and meadows, breathing in silence and admiring the beauty of the world. Most days, he is found either in the garden — pruning roses or discussing potatoes with Uncle Alex — or in his cosy study, surrounded by books.
Dressed in a velvet waistcoat, neat trousers, and carrying an old pocket watch on a chain, he embodies a rare balance of refinement, simplicity, and inner strength.
He is a man who has seen much, understands much, and finally found peace where he belongs — among trees, birds, books, and honest people.

Liumy the Fox Cub

Mischievous and clever. Always inventing games and new adventures.
Liumy the Fox Cub is mischievous, curious, sweet, and incredibly clever. He loves running and playing, asking endless questions — and then, a moment later, sitting very still with a little furrow between his brows, turning the answers over in his mind. Liumy is like a ray of sunshine: lively, quick, and bright-eyed.
He is a loyal friend. He races across the fields with Pippa the Dog, discusses stories with Michel the Rat, and loves nothing more than curling up under a blanket in a chair beside Philomur the Cat. Though still young, Liumy already shows real kindness — and a surprising little spark of wisdom. Liumy is also a natural creator. He is endlessly inventive, forever dreaming up new games, story plots, drawings, and playful adventures for everyone around him.

Pippa the Dog

Faithful and cheerful. Always nearby to protect and play.
Pippa is the puppy of Mr. Vasily Voldemarovich — a mix of Beagle and mongrel. The Beagle is a medium-sized hunting hound, known for its excellent sense of smell and energetic nature. Pippa is white with red and black spots and has long floppy ears.
She is cheerful, lively, and playful — very kind and friendly. She greets everyone with a bright bark, wags her tail happily, and always tries to give a lick.
Pippa is a close friend of the little fox Liumy. Their favourite activity together is playing ball. She is beloved by all the inhabitants of the estate. She loves chasing the chickens but never harms them; instead, she protects them from unwelcome guests and predatory birds.
Pippa embodies loyalty, intelligence, and inner dignity. She enjoys running across the fields with Liumy, then curling up at Philomur’s feet to listen to his stories.
Well-groomed, obedient, and attentive to her friends, she carries a special nobility — not flashy, but genuine. Her presence always brings calm and warmth.

Phil the Gosling

Brave and determined. He never gives up, no matter what.
Phil is a clever, curious, and remarkably brave little gosling. One day, his bright mind and lively spirit caught the attention of a mighty eagle. The predator seized Phil and carried him far away from home. His mother fought desperately to save him, but she could not win the battle.
For many days, Phil lived in the eagle’s high mountain nest, where the bird of prey hoped to raise him as his own heir. Yet Phil never forgot his mother or the poultry yard he called home. The memory of home lived quietly in his heart and gave him the courage to attempt the impossible battle.
At last, he escaped. He wandered through unfamiliar lands for a long time before finally finding his way back to the place where his mother had been waiting for him. He is still young, but he already knows the value of life — and he never gives up.

Peter the Piglet

Quiet and gentle. Dreams of friendship and kindness.
Peter is quiet, shy, and a little timid. Ever since he was very small, the others teased him for his plump pink cheeks and round little belly — calling him names like “doughnut”. It hurt him deeply. Little by little, Peter grew cautious and hesitant: never quick to trust, never quite sure he truly belonged. He often plays on his own — it feels safer that way. After all, it is not his fault that piglets simply look the way they do.
Beneath that timid exterior, Peter is sweet, gentle, and full of kindness. He treasures a small ball and plays with it as if it were his very best friend. More than anything, Peter dreams of finding true friends — those who will accept him exactly as he is. So far, though, it has not quite worked out.
His mother, Sow Martha, once tried to enrol him in ballet school. He was not accepted, and it was another blow to his confidence — another moment of rejection. Yet deep down, Peter was a little relieved: he does not really like dancing anyway. What he truly enjoys is playing ball or digging in the sandbox — that is where he feels most at home.

Uncle Alex

Strong and hardworking. He keeps the land
safe and cared for.
Uncle Alex is a hardworking, good-hearted man — simple in the best sense of the word. Lean but strong, weathered by sun and wind, with rough hands and a face shaped by life itself.
He works long days and never complains. He loves the land, the open air, and the quiet rhythm of ordinary life. He can sit by the pond for hours, fishing in silence, content with nothing but the rustle of reeds and the scent of wildflowers.
Alex says what he thinks and does what he says. He has no patience for lies, tricks, or unnecessary drama. “Life’s too short for scheming,” he often mutters.
At times he can be hot-headed — quick to flare up, quick to speak — sometimes too sharply. But he never hides from responsibility. If he realises he is wrong, he stands straight and tries to set things right. He does not speak in fancy words. He does not need to. His actions are solid and reliable — like a good stone in an old wall.

Auntie Paulina

Kind and caring. The warm heart of the household.
Auntie Paulina is Uncle Alex’s wife — an honest, hardworking woman without a trace of cunning or pretence.
She is the quiet heart of the household. She keeps everything spotless, cooks, launders, scrubs, mends, and somehow manages it all with steady, skilful hands.
For her age, she looks a little older. Years of sun, wind, and endless labour have weathered her face. She has no time for salons or fashionable shops. She dresses simply but neatly — a dark green striped dress, a knitted cardigan, and a clean white apron she washes every single day.
In the mornings, she works at Lord Vasily Voldemarovich’s manor: cooking, cleaning, tending the kitchen, and carrying fresh produce from the market. After work, she returns home — to her vegetable garden, poultry yard, tidy paths, and well-kept sheds. She grows herbs and vegetables, feeds the chickens, gathers eggs, and keeps everything in order.
Aunty Paulina is kind, motherly, and full of quiet compassion. She never complains, though she works from dawn until dusk without rest. In truth, the entire estate — both manor and farm — stands firmly on her shoulders. She does not speak much. She does not need to. Her actions speak clearly: simple, honest, reliable — just like she is.
